# Glossary

<dl class="glossary">
<dt>AAR (after-action review)</dt><dd>A review of what happened and why, after an exercise or battle. In the game: the replay with ground truth (F8).</dd>
<dt>Active infrared (IR)</dt><dd>An old night sight that lights the target with an infrared searchlight. Short range (about 900 m), and the light can itself be seen by the enemy.</dd>
<dt>APDS</dt><dd>Armour-piercing discarding sabot: an older kinetic round (a hard core in a light carrier). Used here by 25/30 mm cannons.</dd>
<dt>APFSDS ("sabot")</dt><dd>Armour-piercing fin-stabilised discarding sabot: a long, thin, very dense dart fired at about 1.5–1.7 km/s. The main anti-tank round of modern tank guns.</dd>
<dt>Assault</dt><dd>The final, fast move onto an objective.</dd>
<dt>ATGM</dt><dd>Anti-tank guided missile (TOW, SK-5, gun-launched RF-1). Long range, shaped-charge warhead, slow flight time.</dd>
<dt>Avenue of approach</dt><dd>A route by which a force can reach an objective.</dd>
<dt>Battle drill</dt><dd>A practised reaction executed without an order (react to contact, break contact …).</dd>
<dt>Battle position (BP)</dt><dd>A defensive location for a platoon, oriented on a threat.</dd>
<dt>Bearing</dt><dd>Direction measured clockwise from north, in mils in this game.</dd>
<dt>Bound / bounding overwatch</dt><dd>A move by one element while another watches over it, then they swap.</dd>
<dt>Branch</dt><dd>A pre-planned alternative ("if contact, go to CP 4").</dd>
<dt>Break contact</dt><dd>The drill for getting out of a fight: smoke, then withdraw.</dd>
<dt>Buttoned up</dt><dd>Hatches closed. Safer, but the crew sees less.</dd>
<dt>CALC</dt><dd>In the database: a value computed with the simulation's formulas, not read from a source.</dd>
<dt>CE (chemical energy)</dt><dd>Penetration by a shaped-charge jet (HEAT, missiles), as opposed to KE.</dd>
<dt>Checkpoint (CP)</dt><dd>A point on a route that the platoon must pass, and where a plan can give instructions.</dd>
<dt>Code word</dt><dd>A pre-agreed word that releases prepared actions when sent.</dd>
<dt>Column</dt><dd>Vehicles one behind another.</dd>
<dt>Combat ineffective</dt><dd>A unit that has lost so much (here 70 % of its vehicles) that it can't fight on.</dd>
<dt>Company</dt><dd>Two to three platoons plus headquarters; the player's command.</dd>
<dt>Composite armour</dt><dd>Armour of layered materials (steel, ceramics …), much better against shaped charges than its thickness suggests.</dd>
<dt>Consolidate</dt><dd>Organise and defend an objective just taken.</dd>
<dt>Contact</dt><dd>An enemy detected and reported; a symbol on your map.</dd>
<dt>Contingency</dt><dd>An order prepared to execute on a trigger (enemy crosses a PL, a time).</dd>
<dt>Counter-attack</dt><dd>An attack by a defender, usually its reserve, against an enemy that has broken in.</dd>
<dt>Dead ground</dt><dd>Ground that can't be seen from a given point.</dd>
<dt>Defilade</dt><dd>Protection from enemy fire or observation by terrain.</dd>
<dt>Delay</dt><dd>Trading space for time: fighting from successive positions and falling back before being fixed.</dd>
<dt>Detection, recognition, identification</dt><dd>The levels of seeing a target: <i>something is there</i>, <i>it is a tank</i>, <i>it is a KURGAN-B</i>.</dd>
<dt>Dismount</dt><dd>Infantry getting out of its carrier to fight on foot; mount is the reverse.</dd>
<dt>Displace</dt><dd>Move from one position to another, usually to an alternate.</dd>
<dt>Echelon</dt><dd>(1) A level of command (platoon, company …). (2) A diagonal formation (echelon left/right). (3) In Soviet-style doctrine, successive waves of an attack.</dd>
<dt>ENDEX</dt><dd>End of exercise: the scenario is over.</dd>
<dt>Engagement area (EA)</dt><dd>Where you plan to destroy the enemy with concentrated fire.</dd>
<dt>ERA (explosive reactive armour)</dt><dd>Explosive tiles on the armour that disrupt incoming shaped charges (and, for heavy types, sabot rounds). Each tile works once.</dd>
<dt>ETA</dt><dd>Estimated time of arrival.</dd>
<dt>Exposed</dt><dd>Fully visible to the threat.</dd>
<dt>FDC (fire direction centre)</dt><dd>The artillery cell that turns a call for fire into firing data.</dd>
<dt>Fire for effect</dt><dd>Artillery firing for real on the target (as opposed to ranging).</dd>
<dt>F-kill (firepower kill)</dt><dd>A vehicle that can't shoot any more.</dd>
<dt>Fix</dt><dd>To pin an enemy in place so it can't move without being destroyed.</dd>
<dt>Flank</dt><dd>The side of a unit or vehicle.</dd>
<dt>Fording depth</dt><dd>The deepest water a vehicle can drive through.</dd>
<dt>Formation</dt><dd>The arrangement of vehicles in a platoon (column, wedge, line …).</dd>
<dt>Fratricide</dt><dd>Firing on your own forces. (Not modelled in this version.)</dd>
<dt>GO / SLOW-GO / NO-GO</dt><dd>Terrain classes for mobility: easy, difficult, impassable.</dd>
<dt>Grid reference</dt><dd>A position as easting then northing (6 digits = 100 m, 8 digits = 10 m).</dd>
<dt>H-hour</dt><dd>The time an operation starts (here: the end of planning).</dd>
<dt>HE / HE-FRAG</dt><dd>High-explosive (fragmentation) rounds, for soft and light targets.</dd>
<dt>HEAT</dt><dd>High-explosive anti-tank: a shaped-charge round. Penetration doesn't drop with range.</dd>
<dt>Hull-down</dt><dd>Only the turret is exposed over cover; the tank can fire.</dd>
<dt>IFV (infantry fighting vehicle)</dt><dd>An armoured vehicle carrying infantry and a cannon, often a missile launcher (WARD M2, SKIF M1). Thin armour.</dd>
<dt>Image intensifier</dt><dd>A night sight that amplifies starlight and moonlight.</dd>
<dt>Intent</dt><dd>The commander's statement of purpose and desired end state.</dd>
<dt>K-kill (catastrophic kill)</dt><dd>A vehicle destroyed outright.</dd>
<dt>KE (kinetic energy)</dt><dd>Penetration by mass and velocity (sabot rounds).</dd>
<dt>LKP (last known position)</dt><dd>Where a contact was last reported; shown with its age in seconds.</dd>
<dt>Loader</dt><dd>The crew member who loads the main gun in Western tanks. Red tanks use an autoloader and have 3 crew.</dd>
<dt>LOGPAC (logistics package)</dt><dd>A convoy that brings a unit ammunition, fuel and other supplies; in the game an instant refill at a set time.</dd>
<dt>LOS (line of sight)</dt><dd>Whether two points can see each other.</dd>
<dt>Masked</dt><dd>Here: a position where the hull is visible but the view towards the threat is blocked.</dd>
<dt>MBT (main battle tank)</dt><dd>A tank designed for all combat roles: BULWARK, KURGAN.</dd>
<dt>MGRS</dt><dd>Military grid reference system. The game uses a simplified local version.</dd>
<dt>Military crest</dt><dd>The line just below the hilltop from which the whole forward slope can be seen.</dd>
<dt>Mil</dt><dd>An angle unit: 6400 mils in a circle; one mil is about 1 m at 1 km.</dd>
<dt>Mission command</dt><dd>Command by intent, with initiative left to subordinates.</dd>
<dt>M-kill (mobility kill)</dt><dd>A vehicle that can't move but may still fire.</dd>
<dt>Multispectral smoke</dt><dd>Smoke that also blocks thermal sights.</dd>
<dt>Objective (OBJ)</dt><dd>An area to seize, hold or watch.</dd>
<dt>On order</dt><dd>A task prepared to be executed when ordered, usually by code word.</dd>
<dt>OPFOR</dt><dd>Opposing force: the enemy (Red).</dd>
<dt>Overwatch</dt><dd>Watching over a moving element, ready to fire.</dd>
<dt>Phase line (PL)</dt><dd>A named line on the map for control and reporting.</dd>
<dt>Platoon (PLT)</dt><dd>Three or four vehicles: the unit you order.</dd>
<dt>Platoon leader (PL), platoon sergeant (PSG)</dt><dd>The platoon's commander (tank 1) and second-in-command (tank 4).</dd>
<dt>Reserve</dt><dd>A force held back to react to events.</dd>
<dt>Reverse slope</dt><dd>The side of a hill away from the enemy.</dd>
<dt>RHA</dt><dd>Rolled homogeneous armour: the steel used as the yardstick for penetration and protection (mm RHA).</dd>
<dt>RPG</dt><dd>A shoulder-fired anti-tank rocket launcher carried by an infantry squad. Short range, deadly to a tank from the side.</dd>
<dt>ROGER, OUT</dt><dd>"Received and understood; end of transmission."</dd>
<dt>Sabot</dt><dd>See APFSDS.</dd>
<dt>Section</dt><dd>Two vehicles: a leader and a wingman.</dd>
<dt>Sector of fire</dt><dd>The area a weapon or unit is responsible for covering.</dd>
<dt>SET</dt><dd>In position and ready.</dd>
<dt>Shaped charge</dt><dd>An explosive that forms a metal jet to penetrate armour (HEAT, missiles).</dd>
<dt>SHOT / SPLASH</dt><dd>Artillery calls: the guns have fired / impact in five seconds.</dd>
<dt>Spot report</dt><dd>A short report of an enemy sighting: who, what, where, direction, distance.</dd>
<dt>Squad</dt><dd>Six to nine infantrymen, the crew of one IFV; one element on the map.</dd>
<dt>Supply point (SP)</dt><dd>A place where platoons drive to be resupplied with ammunition and fuel.</dd>
<dt>Support by fire / attack by fire</dt><dd>Engaging the enemy from a position so that another element can move (support) or to destroy it from there (attack by fire).</dd>
<dt>Suppression</dt><dd>The effect of fire that makes a crew less effective even when it is not hit.</dd>
<dt>TC (tank commander)</dt><dd>The crew member in charge of a vehicle.</dd>
<dt>Thermal imager / thermal sight</dt><dd>A sight that sees heat; works in darkness, dust and most smoke, but not in fog.</dd>
<dt>Thermal crossover</dt><dd>The time around sunrise and sunset when targets and background have similar temperatures and thermal sights lose contrast.</dd>
<dt>Trains</dt><dd>The supply and maintenance vehicles behind a unit, where ammunition and fuel come from.</dd>
<dt>TRP (target reference point)</dt><dd>A recognisable point used to describe targets and trigger actions.</dd>
<dt>Turret-down</dt><dd>Only the commander's sight looks over the cover; the tank can watch but not fire.</dd>
<dt>Weapons control status</dt><dd>FREE / TIGHT / HOLD: what crews may fire at.</dd>
<dt>Wedge</dt><dd>A V-shaped formation pointing forward, the standard for moving in open ground.</dd>
<dt>Wingman</dt><dd>The second vehicle in a section, which follows and supports the leader.</dd>
</dl>

# Troubleshooting

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| The window doesn't open, or the screen stays black | Run `tanks-gl21` instead of `tanks` (older graphics drivers). On Linux, `LIBGL_ALWAYS_SOFTWARE=1 ./tanks` uses software rendering |
| "Cannot find data/ directory" | Keep `data/` next to the executable, or set `TANKS_DATA` to its path |
| The first start of a scenario takes long | The map is being generated and baked. It happens once per map |
| My order does nothing | Check the radio: was it acknowledged (`ROGER, OUT`)? In planning, did you press EXECUTE? Is the platoon halted (**RESUME**)? |
| A platoon doesn't fire | Check **WEAPONS** (HOLD?), the range, and whether its crews can see the target (LOS). A **TURRET-DOWN** or **MASKED** position may not see the threat |
| A right click opened a menu instead of moving | You held the button a little too long (over 0.25 s) or clicked on an object (own unit, contact, graphic, checkpoint) |
| I can't adjust artillery | ADJUST needs observed bursts: impacts within 1.5 km of one of your vehicles |
| The clock doesn't run while I edit | **PAUSE ON MENU** is on (the default): the clock stops while a menu is open or a plan is being edited |
| Options are lost | They are saved to `~/.config/tanks/settings.cfg` (or `TANKS_CONFIG`); check the file is writable |
| Fonts look blocky | **UI FONT** is set to BUILT-IN, or the font file is missing from `data/fonts` |
